    We are always looking for new group members

    Our lab is hiring motivated graduate students and postdoctoral researchers. This page is regularly updated to reflect the availability of research funding. Our research interests lie at the interface between computational materials science and the development of new functional materials for applications in energy storage and conversion. We are looking for industrious and inquisitive candidates who can bring novel, interdisciplinary momentum to the field of materials science and energy storage. We are committed to improving diversity in materials science. Applicants from diverse backgrounds are encouraged to apply.

    Graduate Students

    Graduate students at Purdue University can contact Prof. Canepa directly with inquiries about joining the group. Prospective graduate students should contact Prof. Canepa in advance and subsequently apply for admission to Purdue University by following these instructions.

    Prospective graduate applicants are not required to have prior knowledge of computational chemistry/materials science. However, we do expect some interest in computational science, physics, and chemistry. PhD projects that combine theoretical and experimental research are also very welcome.

    Graduate Study at Purdue

    PhD students in the group are admitted through the School of Mechanical Engineering. Mechanical engineering is the broadest of the engineering disciplines, and the School has more than 90 full-time faculty and research ranging from advanced materials and computational engineering to micro- and nanotechnology. Graduate admissions and degrees are run with Purdue’s Graduate School, which offers more than 160 graduate programs.

    • Funding: every PhD student, and every thesis-based master’s student, in Mechanical Engineering is guaranteed funding, a tuition waiver and a stipend, for the fall and spring semesters while making satisfactory progress. It comes as a research assistantship, a teaching assistantship, or a fellowship (funding options).
    • Degrees: students with a strong bachelor’s record (a GPA of at least 3.6) can enter the Direct PhD without a master’s first; a thesis master’s is also offered, and courses can also be taken in other schools, such as Materials Engineering, Electrical Engineering, or Aeronautics and Astronautics.
    • Applying: applications for fall admission to research programs, and for Purdue fellowships, are due on December 1 (September 15 for spring). They need a statement of purpose, a personal history statement, transcripts, a resume, and three letters of recommendation; the GRE is optional, and application fee waivers are available. The application requirements give the details.

    Domestic US Incoming PhD Students

    US applicants are strongly encouraged to apply for a national graduate fellowship, which pays for their PhD and gives them the freedom to shape their own research project. The NSF, Department of Defense, and Department of Energy computational science fellowships below are applied for in the fall before or at the start of the PhD, so please contact Prof. Canepa early, ideally by September: he is happy to help you prepare your research plan and application. NASA’s program funds space-technology research, including time at a NASA center, and the Department of Energy also offers other internships and fellowships for students and recent graduates. The rules change from year to year, so always check the program’s own page before applying.

    Department of Defense NDSEG Fellowship
    • Who can apply: US citizens, dual citizens, and nationals (permanent residents are not eligible) who have earned, or will earn, a bachelor’s degree by the year they apply, are applying to or within the first two years of a US graduate program, and have at least three years of it left. The research must fit a topic in the Department of Defense’s Broad Agency Announcements.
    • What it provides: up to three years of full tuition and required fees, a stipend, health insurance, and a budget for conference travel.
    • When: the application window opens in early August and closes at the end of October.
    • More: the program’s website.

    Department of Energy Computational Science Graduate Fellowship (CSGF)
    • Who can apply: US citizens and permanent residents who are senior undergraduates or first-year graduate students, and who will be full-time doctoral students; it suits students whose research relies on computing, as in our group.
    • What it provides: up to four years of full tuition and fees and a stipend, a yearly professional development allowance, and a 12-week research stay at a Department of Energy national laboratory.
    • When: the application opens each year in the fall; see the program’s website for the deadline.
    • More: the program’s website.

    NASA Space Technology Graduate Research Opportunities (NSTGRO)
    • Who can apply: US citizens and permanent residents pursuing a master’s or doctoral degree at a US university, whose research can contribute to NASA’s space technologies (energy storage for space missions among them).
    • What it provides: a grant to the university, with the faculty advisor as principal investigator, a NASA scientist or engineer as research collaborator, and a stay at a NASA center as a visiting technologist.
    • When: NASA publishes a call each year on NSPIRES, with the current amounts, eligibility profiles, and deadline.
    • More: the program’s website.

    NSF Graduate Research Fellowship Program (GRFP)
    • Who can apply: US citizens, nationals, and permanent residents who are senior undergraduates, hold a bachelor’s degree with no graduate study, or are in their first year of their first graduate degree (with less than one academic year completed).
    • What it provides: three years of support, used within a five-year period, each with a stipend for the student and an allowance paid to the university in place of tuition and fees.
    • When: applications are due in October, on dates that depend on the field (Materials Research, Engineering, Chemistry, and Physics among them).
    • More: the program’s website.

    International PhD Students

    International applicants apply in the same way and, like every PhD student in Mechanical Engineering, are guaranteed funding while making good progress (see above). They must also show that they are proficient in English.

    English Proficiency Tests

    International applicants whose native language is not English must submit official scores from one of the following tests, no more than two years old, meeting these minimum scores set by the School of Mechanical Engineering:

    • TOEFL iBT: 4.0 overall (80 for tests taken before January 21, 2026), with at least 4.0 in Reading and Writing and 3.5 in Listening and Speaking;
    • TOEFL Essentials: 8 overall and in every section;
    • IELTS: 6.5 overall, with at least 6.5 in Reading, 6.0 in Listening and Speaking, and 5.5 in Writing;
    • Duolingo English Test: 115 overall and in every subscore (Comprehension, Conversation, Literacy, and Production).

    Waiving the English Proficiency Test

    The test is waived for applicants who, within the 36 months before being recommended for admission, earned a bachelor’s or graduate degree from a school that teaches in English in a country where English is the native language (for example, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, Singapore, the United Kingdom, or the Commonwealth Caribbean), and for current Purdue students who have already met Purdue’s English proficiency requirements. The full list of countries and the conditions are on the Purdue Graduate School’s application page. Requirements can change, so please check the School of Mechanical Engineering’s application requirements before applying.

    Postdoctoral Fellows

    Please contact Prof. Canepa for information. Postdoctoral candidates will work with experimental collaborators to guide the prediction of novel compositions of electrode and electrolyte materials for battery applications. Ideal candidates should have:

    • Excellent knowledge of electronic structure methods, including density-functional theory;
    • A deep understanding of solid-state physics/chemistry and physical chemistry in general;
    • Good knowledge of thermodynamics, phase diagrams, and the principles of statistical mechanics;
    • Scientific programming skills (desirable);
    • Basic knowledge of UNIX/Linux operating systems and their command line (Bash, csh, etc.).

    Interested candidates should submit their curriculum vitae directly to Prof. Canepa. Candidates must hold a PhD in materials science, chemistry, chemical engineering, physics, or a related field by the start of employment. Our group is truly multicultural, and there are no citizenship restrictions. Successful candidates can negotiate their salary based on their experience.
